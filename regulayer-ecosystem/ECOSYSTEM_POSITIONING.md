# Ecosystem Positioning

## Purpose

This document defines how the standard maintainer describes itself
relative to the broader ecosystem.

---

## Language Rules

### Allowed

| Phrase | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| "Reference implementation" | One example of a compliant implementation |
| "Maintainer of the standard" | Steward of documentation |
| "One compatible implementation" | Among potentially many |
| "Original specification authors" | Historical fact |
| "Standard documentation host" | Where docs live |

### Never

| Phrase | Why Prohibited |
|--------|----------------|
| "Official" | Implies exclusivity |
| "Certified" | Implies authority to certify |
| "Authoritative" | Implies sole authority |
| "Only trusted" | Implies unique trust |
| "The real" | Implies others are fake |
| "Approved" | Implies approval power |

---

## Positioning Statement

### Correct

> "[Implementation Name] is a reference implementation of the
> Evidence Standard. The standard is open and implementable by
> anyone. Other implementations are equally valid."

### Incorrect

> "[Implementation Name] is the official implementation of the
> Evidence Standard. For certified compliance, use our product."

---

## Relationship to Other Implementations

### Third-Party Implementations

- ✅ Welcome and encouraged
- ✅ Use conformance checklist to verify
- ✅ Run interop tests for compatibility
- ❌ Do not require approval
- ❌ Do not need certification

### Competitive Implementations

- ✅ Healthy for ecosystem
- ✅ Validates standard quality
- ✅ Increases trust
- ❌ Cannot be blocked
- ❌ Cannot be distinguished as "unofficial"

### Open-Source Implementations

- ✅ Explicitly encouraged
- ✅ May use reference code
- ✅ May use test suites
- ❌ No license prevents this

---

## When Describing the Ecosystem

### About the Standard

> "The Evidence Standard is an open specification for verifiable
> decision records. It can be implemented by any party."

### About Implementations

> "Multiple implementations exist. [Our implementation] is one
> reference implementation. Others may be equally valid."

### About the Relationship

> "We maintain the standard documentation. We do not control
> who implements it or certify implementations."

---

## When Competitors Implement

### Expected Response

> "We welcome other implementations. A diverse ecosystem
> validates that the standard is implementable and useful."

### Not Allowed

- "They're not certified"
- "They might not be compliant"
- "Only we can guarantee correctness"

---

## When Governments Mandate

### Expected Response

> "Governments may reference the standard without naming
> any specific implementation. This is intentional and
> encouraged."

### Not Allowed

- Seeking exclusive government endorsement
- Claiming "official government provider" status
- Suggesting alternatives are non-compliant

---

## Marketing Implications

### What Marketing Can Say

- "Reference implementation"
- "Original specification authors"
- "Proven interoperability"
- "Extensive testing"
- "Active standard development"

### What Marketing Cannot Say

- "Only certified implementation"
- "Official provider"
- "Required for compliance"
- "Authoritative source of truth"

---

## Core Principle

> **The standard exists independently of any implementation.**

This means:
- The standard can outlive any company
- No company owns the standard
- No company can prevent implementation
- Value comes from the standard, not from exclusivity

---

## Why This Matters

This positioning:
- ✅ Builds ecosystem trust
- ✅ Encourages adoption
- ✅ Enables competition
- ✅ Prevents regulatory capture
- ✅ Creates long-term value

This positioning prevents:
- ❌ Vendor lock-in accusations
- ❌ Antitrust concerns
- ❌ Ecosystem fragmentation
- ❌ Trust erosion
