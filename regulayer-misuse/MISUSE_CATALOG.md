# Misuse Catalog

**Gallery of Known Misuse Patterns**

This document catalogs observed patterns where Regulayer is used incorrectly to imply properties it does not provide.

## 1. The "Fairness" Feint
**Pattern**: "Our AI is fair and unbiased, as proven by its Regulayer Verification."
**Why it fails**: Regulayer verifies integrity, not logic. A biased decision, recorded faithfully, is still a verified biased decision.

## 2. The Verification Blanket
**Pattern**: "We use Regulayer, so we are compliant with EU AI Act."
**Why it fails**: Compliance requires process, governance, and oversight. Regulayer is just a technical control for record-keeping. It is necessary but not sufficient.

## 3. Cherry-Picking
**Pattern**: A user only exports Proof Bundles for decisions that "look good" and suppresses proofs for bad decisions.
**Correction**: Auditors must request the *Chain* of decisions. If records are missing (sequence gaps), the audit fails.

## 4. The Retroactive Seal
**Pattern**: Trying to sign old logs today to make them look verified.
**Why it fails**: Time Anchoring proves the record did not exist before today. You cannot backdate a blockchain transaction.

## 5. Selective Disclosure (Trust Cherry-Picking)
**Pattern**: User exports only favorable verified decisions, hiding adverse ones, and claims "all our AI decisions are verified".
**Correction**: "Regulayer verification applies only to disclosed artifacts. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence."
**Why it fails**: Courts and regulators require the *full* chain to prove no omission.
